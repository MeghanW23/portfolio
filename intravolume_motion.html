A Slice-by-Slice fMRI Motion
Characterization and Correction Pipeline

Project GitHub

Pharmacological fMRI (phMRI) is the primary tool for understanding how stimulant medications work in the brain to treat ADHD. However, the findings across studies are strikingly inconsistent. A key reason may be hiding in plain sight: stimulants reduce hyperactivity, which reduces head movement in the scanner. This means that "off vs. on medication" comparisons likely capture differences in motion artifact as much as, or more than, real changes in brain activity. The field has acknowledged this as a limitation but no prior study has measured it or corrected for it.


This project directly addresses the motion confound using a novel slice-by-slice motion correction tool. Standard correction methods treat each MRI volume as if it were captured in a single instant, but participants can move within a volume acquisition, and in ADHD, they frequently do. Our algorithm corrects motion at the level of individual slice groups rather than whole volumes, capturing within-volume jitter that standard methods miss. By comparing findings with and without this superior correction, we can separate true treatment effects from motion artifacts and begin to identify accurate neuroanatomical targets for ADHD intervention.


Under the mentorship of Simon Warfield, and in collaboration with his Computational Radiology Laboratory , I am working to characterize and correct head motion artifacts in neuroimaging data from my own lab's ADHD and ASD cohorts in order to reconcile inconsistent findings across the neuroimaging literature, identify reliable neuroanatomical targets for neuromodulatory intervention, and ultimately develop robust biomarkers of treatment response that can guide more precise, personalized care.


This project can be broken down into two parts:



MOTION CHARACTERIZATION


I aim to characterize slice-by-slice level motion in SMS-Accelerated fMRI datasets.

I am implementing and modifying the Computational Radiology Laboratory's motion characterization software toward characterizing slice-by-slice level motion in my own lab's fMRI data from ADHD and ASD participants.

MOTION CORRECTION


The standard way to handle motion-corrupted fMRI data is to censor the affected volumes, but this creates signal discontinuities that can distort results.

The Structured Matrix Completion Approach developed by the Computational Radiology Laboratory reconstructs what the signal should have looked like.

I aim to implement this matrix completion approach to correct for slice-by-slice level motion in my own labs' own data.
