Manuscript in Progress

Walsh, M.E., Auger, J., Balachandrasekaran, A., Gholipour, A., Heras, S., Cohen, A.L. & Warfield, S. (2026). Slice-wise characterization and low-rank recovery of intra-volume head motion in simultaneous multi-slice fMRI.

In simultaneous multi-slice (SMS) functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), head motion occurring between sequential slice-group acquisitions can introduce artifacts within individual volumes that conventional volume-wise correction may not detect.

Here, we developed a pipeline to characterize intra-volume head motion and recover affected slice groups using low-rank matrix completion, comparing its performance with conventional volume-wise motion correction in an attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) pharmaco-fMRI cohort.

We estimated motion at slice-group temporal resolution using rigid-body registration, flagged motion-affected slice groups using a data-driven threshold, and recovered censored data through structured low-rank matrix completion.

We evaluated the resulting data against raw and volume-wise corrected data using temporal signal-to-noise ratio (tSNR) and seed-based functional connectivity, and assessed the prevalence of flagged slice groups among volumes not flagged by conventional framewise displacement (FD), a measure of head motion estimated from successive volume-wise realignment parameters.

Our findings indicate that intra-volume motion is a measurable source of artifact not fully captured by conventional volume-wise methods and that slice-wise recovery may improve data quality while retaining volumes that would otherwise be censored, warranting further validation of its effects on functional connectivity and clinical inferences.

Reference Volume Selection

Reference Volume Selection Script



It is important to select a reference volume that is motion-free, as selecting a reference volume with motion leads to a potentially inaccurate alignment between the reference volume and each slice group it is aligned to.

1. Our reference volume selection script upsamples each 3D volume in the timeseries. For our data's 2.4 mm isotropic voxels, upsampling to 1.236 mm isotropic increases the spatial sampling density, which helps the registration algorithm estimate subvoxel translations and rotations more precisely. This is particularly relevant because the goal is to detect subtle motion between slice groups that may be smaller than the original voxel dimensions.

2. To reduce the influence of ghost artifacts, which appear as spurious signal replicas caused by imperfect MRI acquisition and reconstruction, and unusually high-intensity voxels, which may arise from localized signal anomalies or reconstruction artifacts, voxel intensities in each volume were clipped to the range of 50-1000 arbitrary intensity units (AIU).

3. For each slice group in a given 3D volume, we registered the group to its upsampled, intensity-limited parent volume and calculated its displacement relative to the identity transform of the reference volume, which represents the absence of translation or rotation from the reference volume's original coordinate system.

4. In order to make sure our reference volume is motion-free, we select the upsampled and voxel-limited 3D volume that has the smallest maximum slice-group-to-volume displacement.

Registration Procedure

Main Motion Characterization Script Registration Optimizer Displacement Calculation Script

To characterize intra-volume movement between successive slice-group acquisitions, we performed six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) rigid-body registration of the selected reference volume to each slice group.

An optimization procedure identified the rigid-body transformation that maximized mutual information between the reference volume and each slice group, using statistical dependence between image intensities as a measure of alignment. This yielded six motion parameters for each slice group: three translations along the x-, y-, and z-axes and three rotations about these axes.

We quantified motion between successive slice-group acquisitions by calculating the displacement between their respective motion parameter estimates, producing a motion trace at slice-group temporal resolution. This trace provides finer temporal characterization of head motion than conventional volume-wise estimates and enables detection of motion occurring within individual volumes.

By aggregating slice-group motion estimates at the volume level, we also derived a slice-wise-informed framewise displacement (FD) measure that captures motion between successive acquisitions at a finer temporal scale than conventional FD. This provides a complementary motion metric that can be evaluated independently of slice-group recovery.

Thresholding and Motion Flagging

Motion Thresholding Script

Any 3D volumes containing one or more slice groups determined to have above-threshold intravolume motion is flagged for motion-scrubbing during the motion correction step.

To determine a suitable millimeter threshold, we first calculate a histogram of the number of volumes that exceed a threshold, for each threshold, between the millimeter threshold that flags all volumes, to the millimeter threshold that flags no volumes.

Then, we find the threshold that corresponds to 20% of the data being excluded. From the remaining 80% of volumes, we calculate a mean and standard deviation. We calculate a potential threshold via the following calculation: μ + ( x 2).

If this value is less than 5% of the diameter of a single voxel, we instead use the millimeter value of 5% of the diameter of a single voxel of the threshold. Similarly, if this value is larger than 300% of the diameter of a single voxel, we instead use the millimeter value of 300% of the diameter of a single voxel.

Motion Correction

The goal of our intra-volume motion correction is to recover data corrupted/removed at the slice-group level without discarding whole volumes, using the low-rank matrix completion approach (Balachandrasekaran et al., 2022). In this approach, motion-corrupted slice groups are treated as missing entries in a structured data matrix. Recovery is posed as completion of a super-resolved matrix from the observed (uncensored) measurements. The structured matrix is built from the time series and low rank is enforced to regularize the recovery. Recovery is performed at fine temporal resolution, so slice-timing correction is effectively achieved as part of the reconstruction.

The corrected data is preprocessed with fMRIPrep version 23.1.3 and analyzed with Nilearn's single-subject seed-based connectivity example, unmodified (Esteban et al., 2019, Nilearn contributors, n.d).
